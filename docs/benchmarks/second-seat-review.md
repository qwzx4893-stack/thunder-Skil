# Independent blind comparison: Q4 and Z9

**Verdict: Z9, narrowly.** Its service hierarchy, compact mobile introduction and cohesive work-ticket treatment make the repair-plan task slightly easier to scan. Q4 remains a credible alternative with stronger explicit demo disclosures and an illustration that represents both offered repairs. This is a close comparison of the supplied evidence, not a claim that Z9 has passed interaction or accessibility testing.

## Evidence and scope

Inspected Q4/375.png, Q4/1440.png, Z9/375.png and Z9/1440.png at original image resolution. Read each set's index.html to clarify the local form, validation and confirmation implementation. No runtime interactions, browsing, installs or publication were performed. No parent-directory material, mappings, other runs, maker reports or prior comparisons were read. No condition-identifying leak was encountered.

## Q4

**Strengths**

- The blue introduction and white form create an immediate, legible division between subject and task. On wide screens the form starts high and the numbered steps, summary block and full-width confirmation button establish a clear reading order.
- Original chair and table line drawings directly represent both services. Yellow joint markers give the repair concept specificity without implying photographic proof or measured outcomes.
- The form explicitly states “No live availability or real booking,” marks both prices as demo fees and explains local-only submission beside the action. The footer specifically identifies missing photographs, logo and service imagery and disclaims before/after evidence.
- The screenshot shows the requested services, exact fees and dates, labeled name/email fields, and an unambiguous service/date/fee summary position. The HTML contains corresponding summary updates, local submit prevention, field-specific validation, confirmation markup and an edit control. These are source observations, not verified successful interactions.

**Defects and tradeoffs**

- At 375 px the two-column service choices squeeze “Table stabilization” onto two lines while the chair label stays on one. The fee becomes subordinate small text, making the two choices less evenly comparable than Z9's rows.
- The mobile introduction occupies roughly the first 610 px before the form begins. Its large colored panel, illustration and repeated invitation delay the task more than Z9's introduction does.
- The wide composition is clear but comparatively blunt: the large solid panel and large headline dominate, while the form uses fairly generic bordered options. The small “Repair docket” label contributes less to a coherent furniture-workshop identity than Z9's ticket structure.
- The filled, selected, error and confirmation states are absent from the screenshots, so their visual quality remains unassessed.

## Z9

**Strengths**

- The outlined work ticket, restrained offset shadow, numbered steps and dashed summary dividers form a consistent repair-clinic vocabulary. The annotated chair sketch reinforces hands-on joint repair; the page feels subject-specific without fabricated business credentials.
- Full-width stacked service rows preserve a clear service name, short explanation and aligned price at both widths. In the mobile capture, both service names fit on one line and both fees remain prominent.
- The mobile introduction makes effective use of a small illustration beside the supporting note, bringing the form into view sooner. The wide version balances a generous headline/drawing area against a clearly bounded task panel.
- The date choices, labeled details fields, exact summary categories and primary action are easy to locate. “Choose a demo date” and “Confirm prototype selection” maintain the prototype framing within the task itself.
- The footer honestly identifies the missing real photos, identity and logistics, and describes the illustration as a sketch. The HTML contains local submit prevention, validation, exact service/date/fee mapping, confirmation markup and a change-plan control. Again, this is implementation evidence rather than runtime verification.

**Defects and tradeoffs**

- On mobile the drawing's “Tighten” and “Steady” annotations become extremely small. Its adjacent explanatory note is also noticeably smaller than the main copy, sacrificing some legibility for compactness.
- The local-only submission explanation sits below the action in small text. Q4 communicates the absence of live availability more plainly near the form heading and labels each service price as a demo fee immediately.
- The chair-only illustration gives table stabilization less visual representation than Q4. The wide page also has a relatively large quiet area below the left-hand content, although the task panel remains clearly organized.
- The source leaves its error-summary message visible after individual field-error clearing until a successful submission; this could leave a stale prompt after corrections. This behavior was not exercised and carries little weight in the visual verdict.

## Task evidence and limitations

Both initial-state screenshots visibly support the requested two services, two dates, name/email entry and exact summary categories. Both openly identify the concept/demo medium, avoid invented testimonials, availability counts and environmental metrics, and use illustrative furniture rather than fake before/after photography. Neither screenshot shows an actual selection, failed submission, corrected submission or confirmation.

Both sources contain native form controls, labels/fieldsets, focus styling and reduced-motion rules. Source presence does not establish keyboard usability, screen-reader behavior, contrast conformance or successful preservation of entered details. The local handlers appear designed to retain the existing form rather than reset it on validation errors and to allow editing, but no interaction success, network behavior or accessibility conformance is certified here. No judgment is made about unprovided viewport sizes or states.

Z9 wins narrowly for more consistent service comparison and a more resolved task container across the two supplied widths. Q4's clearer up-front demo language and dual-subject illustration keep the margin small.
