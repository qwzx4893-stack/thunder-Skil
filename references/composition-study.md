# Make the organizing decision visible

Use when a consequential visual or interaction decision is uncertain. A study is a small useful part of the actual interface, not extra ceremony or a polished moodboard. Established patterns and tiny edits can proceed directly.

## State the relationship

Before styling, identify three things in the actual content:

- **Claim or task:** what should the visitor understand or decide next?
- **Evidence:** which object, image, record, comparison or working behavior makes that credible?
- **Action:** what can the visitor do with that understanding?

These are roles, not a mandatory three-section layout. A task table can contain all three; a photographed product may need detail below the opening. Remove an unsupported claim rather than designing a badge around it.

Write one composition consequence: “The full object must remain recognizable before its texture is enlarged,” or “Status, exception and next action must be compared within each row.” “Premium,” “immersive” and a palette are not composition consequences.

## Build the smallest useful slice

Use the target's representative content, longest plausible label, actual media aspect ratio and an important state change. Render at use size on wide and narrow screens. Do not finalize every section before seeing this slice. Avoid dependencies or bespoke effects that do not answer the uncertain question.

Inspect, rather than assume:

| Question | Evidence | Failure response |
|---|---|---|
| What receives attention first? | Actual opening screenshot, text/image proportions, first readable line | Change scale, placement or content order; do not merely increase every heading |
| What proves the subject? | Recognizable object, legible specimen, truthful records or a meaningful state change | Replace decorative filler with real evidence, or honestly change direction |
| Does the text belong to the media? | Real crop and readable text zone at both widths | Recompose or separate text; do not cover the important object with an opacity patch by default |
| Can the visitor act? | Exercise the actual control and inspect its outcome and recovery | Repair behavior or remove the promise; a hover effect is not completion |
| Does density support the task? | Compare, scan and select with representative content | Regroup information; equal units may be correct for comparison |
| Does the narrow view retain the argument? | Inspect order, crop, wrapping and reachable action | Change composition, not only font size |

When two organizations remain plausible, compare them with the same content, viewport and asset quality. Evaluate task fit, hierarchy, subject specificity, feasibility and craft. Do not let one alternative win because it alone has real photographs or working behavior. Explain a consequential choice briefly; do not force user approval when design judgment was delegated.

## Keep, revise, discard

Record the hypothesis, artifact/state, observation and decision in a few lines. The observation must come from the artifact, not the maker's rationale. A simpler treatment can win. If the media or tool is unavailable, label the judgment provisional and identify the check that would settle it; do not report an imagined screenshot as evidence.

Carry the selected relationship into the full surface and recheck it once surrounding sections exist. An attractive isolated opening can fail when the next section repeats its emphasis or delays the task. Remove effects whose cost exceeds their contribution. No fixed number of sections, prototypes, references or animation moves is required.

Inspect how far the core subject or task falls below the opening on a narrow screen. A long introductory block can displace the very artwork, record or comparison the visitor came to inspect. Shorten or reorder around that task while keeping identifying context nearby and reading/focus order coherent. This is a task-dependent decision, not a rule that every image must precede every heading.

Example: for a notebook archive without scans, a readable index of dated demo records can make observation and provenance tangible. Original ruled geometry may organize the metadata; an invented star map must not masquerade as measured positions. A category control should change the record set and survive opening/closing details. On mobile, preserve the observation title, category and inspect action before secondary metadata. The result succeeds through truthful browsing and composition, not through a generic “space” gradient.
