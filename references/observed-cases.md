# Dated observations of original websites

Direct browser inspections on 2026-10-16. Wide view: 1280×720; narrow: 375×812. Read only cases relevant to the decision. These are bounded observations, not endorsements, source-code audits, accessibility certificates or permanently current descriptions. No third-party imagery or code is bundled. Screenshots were inspected during research but not saved into this bundle. Revisit relevant live states before relying on a current detail. Apply references/precedent-lab.md; do not select a brand by copying one case.

## Lusion — capability becomes the medium

Source: https://lusion.co/ — opening and scroll into the next chapter; wide/narrow opening inspected.

**Observed:** a modest claim offset beside the logo/control region precedes a large framed material/3D scene. The next chapter's typography is much larger than the opening claim. On narrow view the claim spans the available width, navigation condenses and the scene becomes a tall composition. Measured wide claim: 32px; next chapter: 128px at this viewport, not prescribed design tokens.

**Interpretation:** the scene demonstrates the studio's offered craft; typography changes scale to mark narrative roles. It is not simply a large headline with a decorative background.

**Transfer:** assign a proof medium and distinguish opening orientation from later emphasis; art-direct the scene's crop/aspect for the device. **Dependency/non-transfer:** bespoke scene, lighting and materials are central; a default floating object does not substitute. Do not require 3D for a product whose value is better explained another way.

**Unverified:** scene engine, device GPU cost, full keyboard access and reduced-motion equivalence.

## Evervault — an explanation the visitor can manipulate

Source: https://evervault.com/ — opening, product diagram and downstream explanation; wide/narrow opening inspected.

**Observed:** centered claim and actions lead into a dark violet product diagram, then modular capability, security and developer explanations. The diagram contains named capability controls. Clicking “Toggle 3D Secure” changed its pressed state to false and dimmed that capability. The transaction illustration continued updating. On narrow view the claim/actions remain readable and the diagram starts below the opening rather than being squeezed alongside it.

**Interpretation:** interaction connects a named capability with a visible representation; the sequence progresses from proposition to mechanism and integration. The illustration is marketing content; inspection does not establish transaction truth or causal rate improvements.

**Transfer:** use a manipulable explanation when a real relationship can be shown; pair it with useful prose and a developer/detail path. **Non-transfer:** encrypted-looking decoration, purple as a technology shortcut, invented transaction metrics or a toggle without a meaningful state change.

**Unverified:** complete simulation logic, keyboard/touch parity and reduced motion. This current page differs from the previously collected Refero reconstruction; historical tokens are not current observation.

## Apple AirPods Pro — the object carries the claim

Source: https://www.apple.com/airpods-pro/ — opening and highlights section, wide/narrow opening inspected.

**Observed:** very large product imagery dominates a controlled light field. Product name, claim, footnote and purchase action have different hierarchy. Highlights follow in a media-led gallery. On narrow view the recognizable product is deliberately cropped near the edges, the claim is centered beneath it and the purchase action moves into a lower band.

**Interpretation:** image scale/crop and sparse copy focus attention on the actual object; subsequent sections carry detail instead of crowding the opening.

**Transfer:** decide how much of an accurate object must remain visible and where explanation/actions belong at each width. **Dependency/non-transfer:** high-quality product media; do not fabricate an accurate product from a generic model or copy product/health claims.

**Unverified:** gallery tab switching (automation did not complete), intended full scroll choreography, accessibility and performance. A large unexpected scroll jump during inspection is not evidence of intended animation.

## Pentagram — identity and navigation can be the same thing

Sources: https://www.pentagram.com/work and https://www.pentagram.com/digital-experiences — gallery/filter wide; Work page narrow.

**Observed:** restrained header and descriptive captions surround varied project imagery. The wide first row uses unequal spans; narrow view becomes a single readable sequence. A compact sentence panel remains near the bottom. Its “Choose work type” control opens categories. Choosing “Digital Experiences” changed the URL, heading and shown project set. The sentence is functional navigation, not decorative microcopy.

**Interpretation:** the firm's work supplies visual variety while site chrome provides continuity; language describes both identity and the browsing model.

**Transfer:** let genuine collection content lead; align category vocabulary, control state and results; preserve readable captions when recomposing. **Non-transfer:** asymmetry as a universal virtue, the firm's categories/logotype, arbitrary labels masquerading as controls. A comparison task can justify equal cards.

**Unverified:** complete keyboard traversal, all category combinations, reduced motion and performance. Rejecting the cookie banner exposed the underlying gallery; it was not a design change.

## Bang & Olufsen A9 — compose the space around the object

Source: https://www.bang-olufsen.com/en/us/speakers/beosound-a9 — opening wide/narrow, cookie overlay dismissed on narrow view.

**Observed:** wide room photography places the speaker to the right and reserves darker space for the claim to the left. A lower band groups scroll cue, price and commerce action. Narrow view uses a tall product/room composition with the claim lower in the frame and a bottom information band. It preserves the physical object's recognizability rather than shrinking the wide frame.

**Interpretation:** lighting, material, negative space and text placement are a joint composition; the room expresses scale and context.

**Transfer:** request/crop photography with text zones and physical scale in mind; specify a mobile alternative when the wide image cannot carry it. **Non-transfer:** exact imagery, pricing, brand copy or a commerce action unsupported by the project. Do not assume the same source image was used at both breakpoints without asset inspection.

**Unverified:** product configuration, gallery behavior, checkout, keyboard and reduced motion.

## Sociotype — the typography is the product

Source: https://socio-type.com/ — opening wide/narrow. Domain verified as the type foundry; similarly named sociotype.com is an unrelated site and was excluded.

**Observed:** a very large full-width wordmark overlays a media field on wide view; narrow view uses a compact “ST” identity and condensed navigation. Family/style controls and specimens expose the subject as usable type. Featured content changed between captures, so its image change cannot be attributed to responsive behavior alone.

**Interpretation:** typography demonstrates the offered material; responsive identity can change form instead of only shrinking.

**Transfer:** make the medium inspectable when it is the product; design explicit compact identity behavior with legitimate brand assets. **Non-transfer:** shrinking all type to fit, appropriating letterforms, or using huge type without content hierarchy. Preserve language coverage, including Arabic where needed.

**Unverified:** specimen control changes, font delivery, complete keyboard path and performance.
