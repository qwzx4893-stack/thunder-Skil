# Generate a direction from evidence

Load for an open visual direction. Preserve explicit references and established systems. The goal is specificity, not novelty in every component. When a consequential relationship is uncertain, use references/composition-study.md to make it visible in a useful slice before elaborating the whole surface.

## Organizing idea

List concrete subject evidence: artifacts, materials, rituals, units, people, places, transformations, constraints. Which changes what the visitor should see or do? Write a concept and its layout consequence. Metaphors are optional; a task-led information model can be the strongest idea.

An archive organized by provenance can expose dates, accession IDs, and relations in a navigable index. A repair service can organize around diagnosis and before/after evidence. Neither needs a decorative dashboard.

Test a similar but different brief. If only the name changes, find stronger evidence or a task consequence. This is a diagnostic question, not an originality score.

## Alternatives when useful

Hold content and viewport constant. Change meaningful axes: information order, focal subject, media relation, geometry, density, type behavior, interaction model. Three palettes on one split hero are one direction.

For consequential ambiguity, render a few small complete compositions with real typography and representative content. Compare at use size. Generated image concepts can aid art direction; verify text, font feasibility, assets, responsive behavior, and interactions before treating them as specifications. Follow supplied references when fidelity is requested.

Select on product fit, hierarchy, distinctiveness, feasibility, and assets. Briefly record why alternatives lost. Do not force approval for taste already delegated.

## Systems with reasons

- Color: sample supplied/verified material or author from subject evidence. Name semantic roles and test real foreground/background pairs. Multiple accents, black, gradients, and vibrant backgrounds can be appropriate.
- Type: prioritize language coverage, reading, tone, weights, and license. One or two families usually suffice; system fonts can suit applications. A new font alone is not a new design.
- Composition: decide focal point, reading order, alignment, media/text relation, and rhythm. Repeated cards suit comparable objects; varied hierarchy needs varied structure.
- Material: observe background field, foreground subject, and their relation. A product stage needs believable scale, contact, light, and shadow. A reading surface may need no texture.
- Motion: state what changes and why. Start static; add cause, continuity, sequence, or spatial explanation.

## Signature when appropriate

An expressive surface may have one organizing mechanism: typographic alignment, crop, navigable diagram, material transition, spatial sequence. State mechanism, communicative purpose, and cost. It may be static and should not monopolize the task.

Dense product UI needs no signature effect. Identity can come from information hierarchy and interaction vocabulary. Familiar controls can stay familiar.

Variance/density/motion dials describe departure from convention, simultaneous information, and temporal expression. Derive them from the task; do not convert them into automatic bans or quality scores.

## Critique the pixels

Run task, similar-brief, and removal tests after rendering. What is understood at first glance? Can the visitor act? Does the subject survive removing decoration? What was specific in prose but generic in the screenshot?

For related projects, retain a compact direction history when useful: actual composition, palette source, type roles, media treatment, mechanism. Compare geometry and tasks, not just heading vocabulary. Diversity must not break a product family.
